Your hair
Not sure? How to tell your curl pattern

Look at your hair after washing and letting it air-dry with no product.

  • 1 — Straight: lies flat from root to tip with no bend. 1A is fine and silky, 1C is thicker with a slight bend.
  • 2 — Wavy: an “S” shape. 2A is loose and stretched out, 2C has defined S-waves that start near the roots and frizz easily.
  • 3 — Curly: springy loops or spirals. 3A is wide like a piece of sidewalk chalk, 3C is tight like a pencil or straw.
  • 4 — Coily: tight coils or zig-zags with lots of shrinkage. 4A coils are about the width of a crochet needle; 4C has a tight zig-zag with little visible curl.

Many people have more than one pattern. Pick the one that covers most of your head.

Not sure? How to tell your porosity

Porosity is how easily your hair soaks up and holds onto moisture.

  • Float test: drop a clean, product-free strand into a glass of room-temperature water. If it still floats after a few minutes it's probably low, if it slowly sinks it's medium, and if it sinks right away it's high. This is a rough guide, not exact.
  • Low: water beads on your hair, it takes ages to get wet and to dry, and products tend to sit on top.
  • High: hair gets wet fast and dries fast, feels dry or frizzy soon after moisturizing, and is often color-treated, bleached or heat-damaged.
  • Medium: somewhere in between and generally easy to manage.
Not sure? Thickness vs. density
  • Thickness is the width of one strand. Roll a single hair between your fingers: if you can barely feel it, it's fine; if it feels like a thread, it's coarse.
  • Density is how many strands you have. Pull your hair back: if you can easily see your scalp it's thin; if you can hardly see it at all it's thick.

You can have fine strands and still have thick, dense hair.
